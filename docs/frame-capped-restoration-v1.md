# Frame-specific restoration diagnostic

The first enriched partner iteration rejects all eight proposals because at least one already-failing frame gets worse. Its shared elastic slack does not encode that frame-specific acceptance rule. The inner optimizer also reaches its iteration limit, so this mismatch is a hypothesis rather than a complete explanation of the observed failure.

The separate `frame_capped_restoration.py` uses one restoration fraction in [0, 1], multiplied by a separate budget for each surface row. For frame depth d, screen s and current whole-clock peak p, the penetration cap is max(d, min(s, p)); the clearance margin is then added to form the row budget. At fraction one, this respects both the per-frame and peak guard budgets for the fixed correspondences. At zero, it requires the original clearance. Joint, root and adjacent-edit constraints remain hard. The seed is rejected if these constraints or frame budgets are already violated. Budgets retain the existing 120 mm ceiling.

Fifteen focused tests pass. A two-frame optimization demonstrates that shared slack can increase a shallower penetration while keeping the peak unchanged; capped slack prevents that in the fixture. Tests also cover independently differenced derivatives, passing-frame peak preservation, unrelaxed constraints, zero surface rows and malformed budgets. These are solver-contract tests, not anatomical evidence.

`reports/frame-capped-restoration-v1` consumes the completed and hashed `projected-full-clock-v1` proof. It reuses all 5,272 frozen initial surface rows, the original 120 controls, objective, five-degree local step bounds and 60-iteration inner budget. It runs one capped inner solve, then recomputes actual geometry at all 30 fitting times and applies the unchanged contact-region and collision guard. It retains solver convergence failures and rejected candidates. No correspondence thinning, new model generation, outer refinement, backtracking or export is part of this diagnostic.

This is distinct from the three-outer-iteration matched projected trial, which remains queued behind its exact predecessor. Even a passing diagnostic candidate still needs refreshed correspondences, full exported geometry, engine checks and independent animator review. All release gates stay open.

The diagnostic completed all 30 fresh geometry samples and rejected its candidate. Peak penetration decreased from 24.7404 to 24.4878 mm, but already-failing frames 66, 74 and 74.5 worsened by 0.0200, 0.1166 and 0.2636 mm. Passing-frame, peak, objective, contact-region, opposing-normal and hard-edit checks passed; per-frame non-regression failed. The event frame also worsened from 1.8928 to 2.4312 mm while remaining below the unchanged 5 mm screen.

The inner solve stopped unsuccessfully after 56 iterations with a positive directional derivative during line search. Its restoration fraction was 0.981932 and its scaled relaxed constraint minimum remained negative (-0.0003449); objective decreased from 1406.6518 to 1284.7598. This does not establish that the frame-cap idea itself is sufficient or infeasible: the numerical solve did not converge, and fixed correspondences do not guarantee fresh geometric constraints. No candidate was adopted or exported. Further work must distinguish convergence error from changing surface correspondences before choosing another correction method.
