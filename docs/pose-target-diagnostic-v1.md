# Why temporal targets remain inaccurate

This is a posthoc diagnostic of retained `pose-trajectory-v1` outputs, not a new generation study or a release evaluation. It asks whether an authored target can be reached within the same anchor edit bounds, and what changes when the neighboring corrected frames are held fixed. It does not change the source candidates or their frozen quality screens.

`scripts/diagnose_pose_targets.py` evaluates the retained anchor, a target-only bounded solve, and a target-only solve with the existing hard neighbor limits. The diagnostic normalizes position and rotation residuals by the study's 5 mm and 5 degree screens. Both probes intentionally omit floor, support and edit-prior objectives. They are not full animation candidates. Local solver failure does not prove infeasibility; success does not establish global optimality or animation quality.

The jump and dance probes each reach essentially zero position/orientation error with box bounds alone, but violate the temporal limits against their existing neighbors. With neighbors fixed, jump position improves from 10.155 to 3.373 mm, but orientation remains 7.868 degrees. Dance position improves from 10.312 to 1.640 mm, but orientation remains 8.349 degrees and anchor floor depth increases from 10.634 to 16.489 mm. Both constrained runs return SLSQP status 8, so their feasible outputs are retained as diagnostics, not convergence evidence.

Getting up reaches 0.000585 mm position error and 0.00362 degrees orientation error with box bounds alone, although that run exhausts its 200-evaluation budget and violates neighbor limits. Its fixed-neighbor probe misses by 47.261 mm and 14.802 degrees and has a small constraint violation; it is rejected. The retained candidate misses by 77.671 mm and 15.687 degrees. None of these diagnostic outputs is promoted.

These results motivate coordinated updates across a time window, with contact and orientation constraints retained, rather than only increasing the current anchor's target weight. They do not prove that the complete requested motion is feasible.

`scripts/verify_pose_target_diagnostics.py` independently reconstructs forward kinematics from saved local poses and probe parameters without importing an optimizer. It checks original hashes, component bounds, root/edit-vector neighbor steps, actual local rotation steps, position/orientation error and full-mesh anchor floor depth. All three reconstructions pass, confirming the recorded failed constraints as well as successful checks. Its first getting-up run found a verifier tolerance-unit mismatch: comparing meters/radians to the dimensionless solver tolerance classified a tiny violation differently. The verifier now independently computes the same dimensionless inequalities and preserves the original rejection threshold; all three cases were rechecked. No probe is exported as an approved animation, and no human review or physical validity is inferred.

Outputs and verification records are in `reports/pose-target-diagnostic-v1/{jump-land,dance}`. The first diagnostic invocation rejected a NumPy scalar weight at the strict request boundary; converting it to a native float fixed the caller. The failed invocation log is retained separately. The production solver and frozen experiment remain unchanged.
