# Conservative fitting-query filter

The existing independent collision audit uses a convex-hull rejection stage; fitting correspondences still queried every vertex in an expanded target bounding box. `hull_surface_correspondences.py` adds the same conservative geometric rejection to fitting, expanding hull planes by the full near-contact margin. A point within that distance of any triangle cannot lie outside the correspondingly expanded hull. The existing numerical plane padding/fallback remains in place.

Retained points use the original signed-distance, closest-triangle, barycentric-coordinate and normal calculations with the same maximum batch of 32 points. The independent full geometry audit and all collision thresholds remain unchanged. This is an opt-in implementation; no live or queued solver dependency is replaced.

Nine focused tests pass across overlapping, touching, separated and near-touching closed meshes, a point just outside a rotated face within the contact margin, and invalid margins. The real verification is `reports/hull-correspondences-v1`: all 30 initial fitting times are compared against the frozen `projected-full-clock-v1` correspondences, then frames 66, 74 and 75 of the completed scaled candidate receive fresh original/filtered comparisons. Source vertex identities, target triangle identities, barycentrics, normals, counts and maximum depths are checked. Timing and point counts are retained separately.

The verification completed all 33 comparisons and 6,156 correspondence rows across the two states. Source vertex identities, target triangle identities and counts match exactly; measured maximum depth, barycentric and normal differences are zero. The expanded-hull filter retains 72,924 of 126,516 bounding-box candidate point queries across this population. All 30 initializer queries took 319.30 seconds; their original reference queries were collected earlier and do not form a contemporaneous timing pair.

The three changed-candidate comparisons timed both routes directly: frame66 takes 18.52 seconds original versus 10.71 filtered (1.73x), frame74 takes 8.82 versus 6.12 (1.44x), and frame75 takes 5.20 versus 2.88 (1.81x). These are single observations on the busy laptop, not a general or whole-solver speed claim. The original live solver studies are unchanged. A faster query is not a motion-quality improvement or a continuous-collision certificate.

The saved candidate correspondences also support `reports/surface-refresh-diagnostic-v1`. At frame66, the one vertex exceeding the frame guard was already constrained, but its nearest triangle changed and its normal rotated 21.30 degrees. The old projection predicts 21.2618 mm while fresh geometry measures 22.0362 mm. At frame74, the one violating vertex keeps its triangle but its closest-point normal rotates 17.83 degrees: old prediction19.9217 mm versus actual20.3593 mm. Both refreshed rows are feasible at the original initializer under its unchanged frame cap. Frame75 gains67 active vertices but has no frame-cap violation. The remaining failing frames66.5/67 are outside this three-frame diagnostic and require their own refreshed rows.

This distinguishes changing closest-surface projections from missing original active vertices in these two observed violations. It motivates an explicit constraint-enrichment experiment: retain original rows and objective, add fresh violating rows only when their initializer feasibility is verified, resolve with the convergent numerical scaling, and recheck actual geometry. Such cuts have not yet been solved or adopted; they do not certify feasibility or final animation quality.
