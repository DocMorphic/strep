# Numerical convergence and fresh contact geometry

The capped restoration diagnostic previously stopped with a positive directional derivative during line search. This experiment keeps the same initial motion, 5,272 fixed surface rows, 120 controls, relative motion/slack weights, hard edit limits and physical acceptance checks. It divides the complete objective and its gradient by 1,000 and changes SLSQP `ftol` from 1e-9 to 1e-12, retaining the 60-iteration limit.

The installed SciPy is 1.15.3. Its [versioned SLSQP documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.15.3/reference/optimize.minimize-slsqp.html) defines `ftol` as an objective stopping precision. The [current documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/optimize.minimize-slsqp.html) explains that several optimality, violation and step checks use this tolerance. Positive rescaling preserves the mathematical relative objective weights, but it can change finite-precision steps and stopping behavior. This is a numerical hypothesis tested on the actual failed problem, not a general solver guarantee. No physical collision threshold is changed.

Before solving, `probe_restoration_derivatives.py` checks six fixed random unit directions and the largest-gradient coordinate at both the initializer and prior rejected candidate. It retains three finite-difference steps per direction (42 comparisons). The predeclared decisive step is 1e-5, with errors divided by max(1, absolute analytic derivative, absolute numerical derivative). All 14 decisive comparisons pass the 1e-5 screen. Maximum objective scaled errors are 3.104e-7 and 4.473e-7; maximum constraint scaled errors are 1.426e-10 and 1.119e-10. The coarser-step errors remain in the report. This is directional evidence, not an exhaustive 120-column Jacobian verification.

The scaled inner solve converges in 45 iterations, with a scaled relaxed-constraint minimum of -1.133e-12, compared with the unscaled solve's unsuccessful 56 iterations and -0.0003449 minimum. Local solve times are 12.21 and 32.11 seconds respectively, measured on the busy laptop; this is not a general timing benchmark.

Fresh geometry still rejects the scaled candidate. Peak penetration rises from the initial 24.7404 mm to 24.7994 mm. Already-failing frames 66, 66.5, 67 and 74 worsen by 0.4108, 0.0590, 0.2030 and 0.0958 mm. Passing-frame, event-contact and objective checks pass, while per-frame and peak non-regression fail. The earlier capped candidate has a lower 24.4878 mm peak but also remains rejected.

The complete 30-time comparison is `reports/restoration-scaling-comparison-v1/comparison.md`. It verifies matched requests, identical budgets and initializer, retained completion hashes, every geometry row and recalculated guard decisions. Nine scaling/derivative tests and twelve protocol tests pass. Source snapshots and both failed candidates are retained. No candidate was adopted or exported, and no release gate is approved.

The result separates numerical convergence from fresh geometry feasibility. The completed [expanded-hull check](hull-correspondences-v1.md) preserves all retained initial rows and three fresh candidate batches. Its saved surfaces identify changed closest-surface projections in the violating vertices at frames66 and74. Both refreshed rows remain feasible at the original initializer, motivating a bounded constraint-enrichment experiment. No enriched solve or repair success has yet been established.
