# Direct evaluation of frozen contact distances

The enriched partner trial has 5,272 surface constraints at its original initializer. Its fractional-frame evaluator previously formed full three-coordinate skin derivatives for source vertices and target triangle vertices, then projected their difference onto each frozen contact normal. Repeated triangle indices and large temporary derivative arrays make that path expensive.

For a fixed correspondence, the signed distance is linear in each actor's world skeleton matrices. `ProjectedTemporalSurface` compiles skin weights, bind-space vertices, triangle barycentric weights, actor placement and the contact normal into two sparse operators. At each evaluation it applies those operators directly to world matrices and their control derivatives. The translation offsets and clearance margin remain explicit. It preserves row order and every constraint; there is no constraint subsampling, changed clearance tolerance or alternative collision geometry. Integer times retain the existing analytic skin path. Correspondences still require rebuilding between outer solver iterations.

`reports/projected-surface-profile-v1` uses the retained actual frame-65.5 batch: 2,230 coupled constraints, 120 controls, initializer and fixed perturbation. All distances and Jacobians match the original calculation within maximum errors of 1.114e-15 m and 3.412e-10 per derivative element. Five cold-cache repetitions give 23.29–32.28× speed ratios over the original evaluator and 10.91–16.35× over unique-vertex evaluation. The two sparse operators contain 149,700 nonzero entries and occupy 1,814,248 bytes in their recorded arrays. That size is not a measured peak-process-memory reduction.

These timings apply to one constraint batch on the busy laptop. They exclude surface queries, operator construction, constrained optimization, export and full geometry audits. They do not establish a whole-solver speedup or better animation.

`reports/projected-full-clock-v1` has reconstructed and frozen correspondences at all 30 fitting times. It compares the original and projected evaluations at two control states and two clearance margins, checks the retained witness counts/depths, and records each frame's construction and evaluation time. All 120 comparisons now pass, including empty batches, across 5,272 constraints. Maximum distance error is 1.679e-15 m and derivative error is 3.412e-10. Summing the per-frame median evaluations gives 0.8806 seconds for the original and 0.09780 seconds for the projected route (9.004x). Operator construction took 0.190 seconds; the separate correspondence queries took 606.27 seconds. This is not a measured full-optimization speedup.

The separate `projected-surface-path-v1` trial has consumed the complete projection proof and now waits for the exact enriched export/geometry owner. Its frozen sources, initializer, fitting clock, objective, root/joint limits, selection screens and three-iteration/60-inner-iteration budget match the existing trial. Only derivative/distance evaluation changes. Numerical agreement can still lead to different nonlinear optimizer steps; full exported results must be compared. The current solver is unchanged.

The trial's automatic completion worker checks decoded bounds, 600 actual engine actor-frames and 299 integer/half-frame samples for both original and candidate scenes. `projected-partner-comparison-v1` then validates the matched protocol and records newly failing, cleared and worsened samples, both peaks and floor curves. Root and leg motion stay fixed, so this arm-only experiment cannot fix the existing floor failure. No quality or release approval is implied.

Twenty-one focused tests pass: direct projection against an independent loop-based skin calculation, directional derivatives, both integer/fractional paths, repeated triangle vertices, frozen record ownership, empty constraints, malformed inputs and changed comparison inputs/limits/budgets. Source snapshots and test hashes remain separate from the pending model-quality evidence.
