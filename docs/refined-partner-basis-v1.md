# Finer timing controls for partner collision correction

Development experiment, 2026-09-28. The project-wide goal remains active. The study is complete and independently verified; its numerical improvement does not pass the motion-quality screens.

The prior high-five correction reaches its contact pose but retains forearm overlap during the approach: 24.047 mm peak partner depth and 11.959 mm peak floor depth. Earlier frame-preserving correction improved the approach only slightly. An exploratory solve on its older frozen planes predicts 23.556, 22.719 and 22.467 mm remaining overlap with 1°, 3° and 10° trust radii. These predictions suggest testing the temporal control representation; they do not prove nonlinear infeasibility or identify a global optimum. The probe output and exact input hash are retained separately.

The new study starts from the completed buffered candidate in `reports/frame-guarded-partner-v2`. It inserts control knots at 65, 67.5, 70, 72.5, 77.5, 80, 82.5 and85 into the original five-knot basis. Piecewise-linear interpolation embeds the old controls in the new basis. There are now312 scalar controls instead of120. The original contact frame75 remains an exact locked knot, the fade window and both roots remain unchanged, and the same per-joint rotation budgets and5° adjacent edit-vector limit apply. The purpose is to allow different approach and recovery shapes without relaxing physical edit limits.

All299 integer/half-frame skeleton poses agree with the old representation to6.67e-16 matrix error before any optimization; contact-event controls are exactly equal. This verifies insertion, not the quality of a future proposal. Nineteen component tests pass, including an opposing-plane example that the coarse basis cannot improve but the refined basis can, without changing event or limits. Export validation now reconstructs the declared basis and checks its control budgets instead of assuming exactly five knots. Both old and refined protocols are tested; old immutable artifacts remain unchanged.

`reports/refined-partner-basis-v1/request.json` freezes the inputs, implementation snapshots,30 fitting times, trust radii3°/1°/0.25°, and at most three buffer attempts per radius. Fresh full meshes build the initial planes. Each proposed candidate is checked against the original per-frame allowance, `max(5mm, warm depth)`; previously failing samples get only the same1nm numerical comparison allowance. Violations add a proposal-only buffer of1.5 times the observed excess. Smaller trust regions reset that buffer. A candidate must pass all fitting times and improve the peak by at least1micrometre before export. Unvisited frames in rejected attempts are not reported as checked.

Conditional export verifies actual rotation/root bounds, protected context, source hashes and Godot import for600 raw/candidate actor-frames. Full exported geometry is then measured at299 times for both scenes and compared to the prior buffered scene. The final report must expose any regression outside the fitting clock, changed floor curve, event defect or failure to reach5mm clearance. Numerical improvement alone does not establish anatomical plausibility, convincing reactions or realism. Root/leg motion is fixed, so floor repair is outside this particular intervention.

`compare_partner_basis_linearization.py` provides a matched diagnostic after the measured linearization exists: it maps the original five-knot subspace into the refined controls and solves against identical warm motion, planes, caps and trust radii. This distinguishes extra temporal freedom from merely taking another optimization step. Its predictions still require fresh geometry.

## Source guidance rechecked

NVIDIA documents sparse spatial guidance and approximate constraint following, with post-processing recommended for contact accuracy. That supports evaluating actual exported surfaces independently; it does not make this deterministic solver a trained scene-aware model. [Kimodo constraints](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/kimodo/docs/key_concepts/constraints.html), [best practices](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/kimodo/docs/key_concepts/limitations.html).

The Uni-Inter author repository still describes a locally trained checkpoint and identifies a noncommercial source license. It remains research context rather than an acquired product replacement. The prior pinned-source inspection is in [interaction research v2](interaction-research-v2.md). [Author repository](https://github.com/Darkdawner/Uni-Inter), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13032). No new code, weights, datasets or training have been adopted from that work.
