# Body correction with an explicit timed hand layer

The target preflight found that Kimodo's SOMA30 representation drops authored finger rotations. The chosen authored contact pose passes its event geometry checks, but its relaxed-hand expansion does not. This study therefore keeps body correction and finger authoring as two explicit operations. It does not claim that the checkpoint generates the authored hand pose.

`study_paired_pose_posture.py` uses every body-guided seed from the preceding comparison: 1301, 2089, 3253, 4099 and 5101, with both actors. The fixed variants are raw generation, deterministic body correction, and the same body correction followed by timed finger authoring. Raw sources and scene placement are retained. No inference or training occurs. The separate hand-guided population is not part of this experiment.

The pinned native motion-correction component fits full-body target frames 0, 75 and 149 from `paired-contact-target-plan-v3`. Solver foot-contact inputs are zero; the unchanged model-predicted labels remain in the output and are not verified contact. Every native correction is repeated and compared bit-for-bit, with input-tensor preservation checked. This fits the sparse body anchors; it does not certify continuous contact, anatomical limits or motion quality.

The existing `hand_posture.py` authoring component then blends the hash-bound target's left finger rotations over frames 60–90, reaching full strength at frame 75. Its declared limits are 60 degrees total edit and 5 degrees change in correction per frame. The body root, non-finger local rotations and saved contact labels are preserved by this second layer. The measured maximum finger edit is 34.461 degrees; the maximum adjacent correction change is 3.441 degrees. Matching an authored target does not establish that the target is anatomically appropriate.

All 30 actor clips (ten raw controls plus twenty corrected variants) are exported and verified in actual paired Godot scenes: 4,500 actor-frame samples. Independent saved-array and decoded-anchor checks cover every actor. Maximum decoded finger-matrix error against the authored target is 2.85e-8. Maximum local-matrix error outside the hand interval is 3.58e-7. The largest body-fit root displacement is 29.56 mm; the largest body joint change is 68.03 degrees at the left wrist of seed 2089 actor A. These are measurements, not an accepted anatomical or body-edit budget. No variant is approved.

`audit_paired_pose_posture.py` runs a separate complete surface audit: all 150 integer frames and every half frame from 60 to 90, 180 samples per scene. It tests both actor directions for vertex penetration, full-skin floor depth, designated palm distance, and distributed palm contact at the event. Raw geometry evidence is reused only after exact mesh-hash, placement, sample-population and proof-hash checks against the completed preceding audit. Corrected variants are decoded and measured afresh. The audit is running; engine-import success and edit preservation do not substitute for it. Vertex samples do not certify continuous collision, edge-only triangle crossings or self-collision.

Artifacts: `reports/paired-pose-posture-v1`, `reports/paired-pose-posture-audit-v1` and the [three-stage viewer](http://127.0.0.1:8767/reports/paired-pose-posture-review-v1/viewer.html). The viewer retains a shared half-frame clock across all fifteen seed/variant choices. It displays correction provenance and pending geometric validation explicitly. Independent animator review and cleanup-time evidence remain absent; release gates remain open.

The first completed corrected scene, body-fit seed 1301, still has 21.470 mm whole-clip penetration and 10.664 mm floor penetration. The authored-hand version's separate event preflight passes the fixed event screens: 22.623 mm designated point distance, 1.897 mm penetration, no floor penetration, distributed region witnesses above the required area, and 5.199 degrees opposing-normal error. Its whole-motion audit is pending. This is an event-pose improvement, not a successful high-five clip.
